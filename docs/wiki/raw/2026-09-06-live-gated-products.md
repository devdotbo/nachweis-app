# Live gated products: restriction, mechanism, portability, plug-in interface

Question: for products that are live today and restrict who may use them, how is the restriction implemented, what must a user do, and would a portable wallet-bound eligibility attestation (issued once from a state ID wallet or a passport proof, readable by any contract) be accepted as a plug-in, and by whom.

Evidence labels: FACT (read at the source, URL and date given), COUNT (measured, what was counted stated), CLAIM (secondary source), OPINION. "Unverified" means looked for and not confirmed. All fetch dates are 2026-09-06 unless stated.

## Headline findings

1. FACT: exactly two live products publish a wallet-bound eligibility attestation that any contract can read: Coinbase Verifications (EAS on Base) and Kraken Verify (EAS on Ink, same contract design). Both have exactly one attester each (COUNT via EAS GraphQL: Base Verified Account 722,434 attestations, 1 attester; Ink Kraken Verify 25,264 attestations, 1 attester). Both resolvers expose an attester allowlist controlled by the exchange's admin role. Neither has an issuer onboarding process; Coinbase's help page says third-party issuance is a "goal".
2. COUNT of live products where an on-chain portable attestation is evidenced as consumed on chain by more than one independent app: 0. Coinbase Verifications is the closest: Coinbase's own directory lists 11 third-party destinations plus 2 "coming soon", but on-chain enforcement is evidenced only for Coinbase's own Verified Pools; every other consumer's check mode (contract, indexer or front end) is unverified.
3. FACT: a pluggable verifier interface where adding an issuer is a config change exists in three places: Uniswap v4 Permissioned Pools (IAllowlistChecker, set by the token issuer's adapter owner; zero adapters live on the documented mainnet factory), ERC-3643 (TrustedIssuersRegistry.addTrustedIssuer, called by the token's registry owner) and Chainlink ACE (ITrustedIssuerRegistry.addTrustedIssuer, private beta, hosted onboarding). In all three the switch is thrown by a regulated operator, not by the attester.
4. FACT: the tokenized-stock products that are open to EU retail (xStocks by Backed, Ondo Global Markets, Robinhood Chain Stock Tokens, Dinari dShares, Midas) restrict only the primary market (issuer KYC, wallet allowlist for mint and redeem) and run a blocklist or sanctions check on secondary transfers. There is no holder allowlist to plug into. Robinhood EU's own app tokens are non-transferable and gated in app. Spiko and Franklin are the EU money market funds with a strict holder allowlist, both run by the fund's own onboarding.
5. FACT: Polymarket and Hyperliquid enforce their exclusions by IP geolocation at the front end and API; no on-chain eligibility check, no attestation pilot found (COUNT 0).
6. Exchange self-hosted wallet proofs: COUNT 5 of 5 core EU exchanges (Kraken, Bitpanda, Bitvavo, Coinbase, BISON) accept a signed message and store the verified address for reuse inside that exchange; COUNT 0 accept a proof made at another VASP.
7. EUDI wallet, ID Austria or German eID in crypto onboarding: one production hit, BISON (Boerse Stuttgart Digital) verifies German residents with the German eID (Online-Ausweisfunktion, NFC, PIN) via IDnow in the BISON app. Scalable Capital offers POSTIDENT with Online-Ausweis for securities account opening (crypto only as ETPs). No EUDI wallet, ID Austria, itsme, MitID, FranceConnect or SPID use by any of the 20 named CASPs, custodians or banks was found. No large-scale pilot (POTENTIAL, EWC, NOBID, DC4EU) had a crypto or CASP use case. Nothing found via 9 free portal queries (negative sweep) and 6 paid search requests.

## A. Coinbase Verifications and Coinbase Verified Pools (Base)

Restriction. FACT (Coinbase help, onchain verification article): any user with a Coinbase retail account "not subject to any restrictions that would prevent buying, selling, and/or converting crypto", or a Prime business account, may attest up to 3 wallets, free of gas, revocable by the user after 24 hours, Base only. Excluded: everyone without a Coinbase account.

Mechanism. FACT (git clone of https://github.com/coinbase/verifications, last commit 831a0d2 of 2024-08-15, read at [local path, withheld]):
- Schemas on Base mainnet: Verified Account 0xf8b05c79f090979bf4a80270aba232dff11a10d9ca55c4f88de95317970f0de9, Verified Country 0x1801901fabd0e6189356b4fb52bb0ab855276d84f7ec140839fbd1f6801ca065, Coinbase One 0x254bd1b63e0591fefa66818ca054c78627306f253f86be6023725a67ee6bf9f4; the subagent also found undocumented Business Account and Business Country schemas created 2025-02-13. Attester 0x357458739F90461b99789350868CD7CF330Dd7EE, indexer 0x2c7eE1E5f416dfF40054c27A62f7B357C4E8619C, resolver 0xD867CbEd445c37b0F95Cc956fe6B539BdEf7F32f, EAS predeploy 0x4200000000000000000000000000000000000021.
- Consumer side: AttestationAccessControl.onlyAttestation(bytes32 schemaUid) calls IAttestationIndexer.getAttestationUid(msg.sender, schemaUid), then IEAS.getAttestation(uid), then AttestationVerifier.verifyAttestation(attestation, recipient, schemaUid), which checks recipient, schema UID, expiry and revocation. It does not check the attester address. Trust rests on the schema being resolver-protected.
- Issuer side: CoinbaseVerificationsResolver.allowAttester(address) and removeAttester(address), both onlyRole(DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE); public mapping allowedAttesters. Indexer.index(uid) is onlyRole(INDEXER_ROLE), held by the resolver.
- FACT (eth_call to Base mainnet, selector 0x6b248080): allowedAttesters(0x3574...7EE) is true.
- COUNT (EAS GraphQL groupByAttestation by attester): Verified Account 722,434 attestations, 1 distinct attester; Verified Country 306,438, 1 attester; Coinbase One 173,433 (subagent). 407,854 Verified Account attestations are revoked (56 percent; Coinbase revokes and reissues on status change). Latest Verified Account attestation was issued about 100 minutes before the query, so issuance is active. Enumeration of AttesterAllowed events failed on every free RPC (10,000 block range limit) and Etherscan V2 needs a paid plan for Base, so "ever allowed" is unverified; the attestation data shows no second attester in practice.

Who holds the data. Coinbase, Inc. On chain in plaintext: wallet address, boolean, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country string, timestamps and permanent revocation history.

Portability. FACT (https://www.coinbase.com/onchain-verify, read via Parallel Extract): directory lists Base Discord (Guild), Verified Pools, Veil Cash, Singularity, Basepaint, Superchain Dev Console, Talent Protocol, DeForm, Perennial, Icebreaker, Gitcoin as "Use on", and Volmex and Index Coop as "Coming soon", plus Coinbase One benefits (Base, Aerodrome). COUNT: 11 "Use on" third-party entries, 2 "Coming soon". FACT (Wayback snapshot 2025-09-26 of https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/verifications/introduction/use-cases; the live URL returned 404 today): Coinbase named Optimism Superchain faucet, Perennial, Deform, Talent Protocol, Volmex, Index Coop as current use cases. Subagent A adds Morpho RWA vaults by Steakhouse and Re7 (Centrifuge blog 2024-08-27) and 0x/Matcha routing into Verified Pools; the Re7 vault contract is a plain MetaMorpho with no attestation reference, so its gate is front-end or curator level (OPINION). COUNT of consumers with on-chain enforcement evidenced: 1 (Verified Pools). No consumer does US exclusion; Verified Pools' country allowlist includes the US.

Coinbase Verified Pools. FACT (subagent A, verified source on Blockscout): Coinbase-run Uniswap v4 pools on Base (EURC/USDC, ETH/USDC, cbBTC/USDC, cbBTC/ETH, cbETH/ETH), Gauntlet-configured. VerifiedPoolsBasicHook 0x5cd525c621afca515bf58631d4733fba7b72aae4 resolves the human sender via IMessageSender(router).msgSender() and calls IHookPolicy.verify(sender, data). BasicPolicy 0xcC7fE2Cf5B79F3EFaBF42caC8E223813242E1454 = VerifiedAccountPolicy AND VerifiedCountryPolicy AND NonSanctionedPolicy (Chainalysis list 0x3A91A31cB3dC49b4db9Ce721F50a9D076c8D739B). Live config read via RPC: isVerifiedCountry true for US, NL, SG; false for DE, GB. beforeInitialize reverts for non-allowlisted initialisers, so third parties cannot attach the hook to their own pools. Live as of today; no deprecation found.

What it would take to accept an external attestation. Interface exists: yes (allowAttester on the resolver, and schema setters on BasicPolicy). Issuer onboarding process: no. FACT (help page): "Our ultimate goal is to enable 3rd parties to issue verifications using our service." COUNT of external attesters ever: 0. The alternative that needs no Coinbase decision: register an own EAS schema on Base (permissionless), deploy an own indexer implementing the two-function IAttestationIndexer, and have each consumer add the schema UID; that is a per-consumer code or config change (BasicPolicy exposes two schema slots per check via setters; a third needs code). The user must have a Coinbase account for the existing schemas; there is no path for a non-customer.

2026 status. Repo last pushed 2024-08-14, not archived; CDP developer pages for Verifications now redirect to the generic docs index (deliberate removal unverified); still branded "Verifications"; no rebrand or deprecation found.

## C. Uniswap v4 Permissioned Pools (Superstate, Securitize, Dowgo)

FACT (clones of Uniswap/v4-periphery and Uniswap/v4-hooks-public, docs and the 2026-07-23 blog post, subagent B):
- PermissionedHooks implements beforeInitialize, beforeSwap, afterSwap, beforeAddLiquidity. _beforeSwap and _beforeAddLiquidity call _verifyAllowlist, which for each currency that is a verified PermissionsAdapter calls IPermissionsAdapter.isAllowed(sender, SWAP_ALLOWED | LIQUIDITY_ALLOWED) and allowedWrappers(router), reverting Unauthorized. Removing liquidity is deliberately not gated.
- The adapter stores no allowlist. isAllowed is allowListChecker.checkAllowlist(account, PERMISSIONED_TOKEN) & permission == permission.
- Exact plug-in interface: interface IAllowlistChecker is IERC165 { function checkAllowlist(address account, address tokenAddress) external view returns (PermissionFlag); } with PermissionFlag = bytes2, SWAP_ALLOWED = 0x0001, LIQUIDITY_ALLOWED = 0x0002. Set at PermissionsAdapterFactory.createPermissionsAdapter(IERC20 permissionedToken, address issuerAdmin, IAllowlistChecker allowListChecker); changed by PermissionsAdapter.updateAllowListChecker(IAllowlistChecker) onlyOwner (ERC-165 check). Docs: the checker can be "a mapping you write to, an external registry, a merkle root". Swapping in a third-party attester is a config change by the adapter owner, that is the token issuer.
- FACT: blog, four doc pages, both repos and the uniswap-ai plugin contain zero mentions of Keyring, zkMe, Chainlink ACE, Coinbase Verifications, ERC-3643 or any named provider. The example checker is an owner-written mapping.
- COUNT (Blockscout, 2026-09-06, indexed to block 25919653): 0 transactions and empty logs on the documented mainnet factory 0x7DA9...6452, hook 0x499a...28c0, position manager 0x63Bd...1973, and the Sepolia factory. Factory deployed 2026-08-11. Caveat: an issuer could use a private factory instance. Uniswap's trade API has a /v1/permissions endpoint returning isAllowlisted plus kycUrl and issuer for non-allowlisted users.
- Partners: Superstate fills its allowlist through its own KYC and investment agreement, qualified purchasers only for the funds, admin-written AllowlistV3 (mainnet 0x02f1fa8b196d21c7b733eb2700b825611d8a38e5, function isAddressAllowedForPrivateInstrument per subagent A); whether a checker reads it: unverified. Securitize: BUIDL trades via UniswapX RFQ with Securitize whitelisting (Feb 2026), retail excluded; no v4 pool or checker found. Dowgo: professional investors only (MiFID II), ERC-3643 tokens, ACPR authorisation pending, "not yet authorized and operates no trading platform"; its ERC-3643 checker is in no public repo. No partner added since July 2026 found.

Who holds the data: the issuer. Portable: per pool no; the checker is the portability layer and can read any registry. Plug-in: interface yes, issuer onboarding is the token issuer's decision (config), Uniswap itself has no say and no process.

## D. ERC-3643 in production (Tokeny, T-REX, ONCHAINID)

FACT (clones of ERC-3643/ERC-3643 4.1.3 and onchain-id/solidity, docs.erc3643.org, docs.tokeny.com, subagent B):
- Transfer requires IdentityRegistry.isVerified(address) for the receiver plus compliance modules. isVerified loops the required topics from ClaimTopicsRegistry; for each topic it gets TrustedIssuersRegistry.getTrustedIssuersForClaimTopic, reads identity.getClaim(keccak256(abi.encode(issuer, topic))), and calls IClaimIssuer.isClaimValid(identity, topic, sig, data), which recovers the signer, requires a CLAIM-purpose key (purpose 3) on the ClaimIssuer and no revocation.
- Claims are written by the holder: Identity.addClaim(uint256 _topic, uint256 _scheme, address _issuer, bytes _signature, bytes _data, string _uri) is onlyClaimKey and itself checks isClaimValid.
- Plug-in interface: TrustedIssuersRegistry.addTrustedIssuer(IClaimIssuer _trustedIssuer, uint256[] calldata _claimTopics) external onlyOwner (max 50 issuers, 15 topics each), and ClaimTopicsRegistry.addClaimTopic(uint256) onlyOwner. Adding an attester is one transaction by the registry owner (the token issuer or its agent). The attester must be an ERC-734/735 ClaimIssuer contract with a signing key; an EAS attestation or a ZK verifier would need a wrapper contract implementing isClaimValid.
- Who issues claims in production: FACT (docs.tokeny.com): "Tokeny, as a software provider, does not act as a KYC agent"; issuers appoint KYC agents; named vendors Sumsub and Onfido; onboarding portals Tocan, Delio, Platform Eleven, Globacap. Qualification is signed by "you or your compliance officer" in Tokeny's app. Holder journey: register in the issuer's Investor App, KYC, ONCHAINID deployed or linked, claim issued, wallet registered with country code.
- Reuse: by design one ONCHAINID per holder, claims keyed by (issuer, topic), reusable by any token whose registry trusts the issuer. FACT quotes: Tokeny "Reuse your verified profile from investment to investment"; ONCHAINID docs "Generic Claims ... shareable between DeFi protocols and between tokens of multiple issuers". COUNT of production instances where one issuer's claim is trusted by a second, independent issuer's token: 0 found (Tokeny docs show existing ONCHAINIDs re-added to a new token's registry inside the Tokeny platform). The Task pro second opinion reached the same result.
- Scale: COUNT 151 members listed on erc3643.org/members (counted by CSS class). FACT: erc3643.org and tokeny.com state "> $32 billion tokenized", Tokeny "120+ customers". Live token count unverified. EU names with URL: ABN AMRO green bond (2023), 21X (BaFin DLT TSS, live 2025-09-08, ERC-3643 per CLAIM), BX Digital, NPEX, FundsDLT, Bitbond, Alastria, InTech, Deloitte France; Apex Group owns a majority of Tokeny.
- EUDI, eIDAS, eID: zero matches in the three repos and all ERC-3643, Tokeny and ONCHAINID pages fetched. Only adjacent item: CLAIM that InTech S.A. and LuxTrust built EDDITS linking eIDAS identity to ERC-725/735.

## E. Aave Horizon

FACT (clone of aave-v3-horizon, docs, subagent A): launched 2025-08-26 on Ethereum mainnet, pool 0xAe05Cd22df81871bc7cC2a04BeCfb516bFe332C8. ### 2. Three most plausible plug-in points for a new attestation issuer, ranked by "an interface exists and adding an issuer is a config change"

1. Uniswap v4 Permissioned Pools. Interface: IAllowlistChecker.checkAllowlist(address account, address tokenAddress) returns bytes2 (SWAP_ALLOWED 0x0001, LIQUIDITY_ALLOWED 0x0002), ERC-165. Wiring: PermissionsAdapterFactory.createPermissionsAdapter(IERC20 permissionedToken, address issuerAdmin, IAllowlistChecker checker) or PermissionsAdapter.updateAllowListChecker(IAllowlistChecker) onlyOwner. Who throws the switch: the token issuer that owns the adapter; Uniswap has no gate and no process. State: contracts live on mainnet since 2026-08-11, COUNT 0 adapters and 0 pools observed. An attestation issuer can deploy a checker that reads its own registry today; getting a real issuer's adapter to point at it is that issuer's commercial decision.
2. ERC-3643. Interface: TrustedIssuersRegistry.addTrustedIssuer(IClaimIssuer _trustedIssuer, uint256[] calldata _claimTopics) onlyOwner, plus ClaimTopicsRegistry.addClaimTopic(uint256) onlyOwner; the issuer must be a ClaimIssuer contract whose isClaimValid(identity, topic, sig, data) verifies a purpose-3 key; the holder calls Identity.addClaim(topic, scheme, issuer, signature, data, uri). Who throws the switch: the token's registry owner (issuer or agent). State: 151 association members, "> USD 32bn" tokenized (CLAIM), production issuers use Tokeny's platform with Sumsub or Onfido as KYC agents. A wrapper ClaimIssuer around an EAS or ZK attestation is a small contract; the topic and issuer additions are config.
3. Coinbase Verifications and Kraken Verify (EAS). Interface on the issuer side: CoinbaseVerificationsResolver.allowAttester(address) onlyRole(DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE) on 0xD867CbEd445c37b0F95Cc956fe6B539BdEf7F32f (Base); the Ink equivalent unverified. Who throws the switch: Coinbase's admin (or Kraken's); no process, 0 external attesters ever, help page calls it a goal. Without them: register an own EAS schema on Base or Ink (permissionless), deploy an IAttestationIndexer (index, getAttestationUid), and ask each consumer to add the schema UID; consumers built on AttestationAccessControl check schema, recipient, expiry and revocation but not the attester, so a consumer that points at the new schema and indexer accepts the new issuer with a one-line change. Coinbase's own BasicPolicy exposes two schema slots per check as setters.
Chainlink ACE has the cleanest issuer registry (ITrustedIssuerRegistry.addTrustedIssuer(string issuerId, bytes context)) but is private beta with hosted onboarding and 0 production consumers, so it ranks below the three.

### 3. Would any live product accept a state-ID-derived attestation without a commercial agreement

No. In every live product the acceptance decision sits with a regulated operator or the platform owner: the token issuer (Uniswap adapter owner, ERC-3643 registry owner, Aave Horizon issuer, Spiko, Franklin, Superstate, Securitize), the exchange admin (Coinbase, Kraken resolver allowlists), or the vendor (Keyring key registration, zkMe operator role, Chainlink hosted onboarding). None documents an issuer onboarding process; none names an external attester in production. The tokenized-stock secondaries (xStocks, Ondo, Robinhood Chain, Dinari, Midas) do not check eligibility for secondary holders at all, so there is nothing to plug into; their primary market is issuer KYC with a wallet whitelist for mint and redeem. Polymarket and Hyperliquid have no on-chain check and have piloted no attestation. What is accepted without agreement is limited to permissionless registration: an own EAS schema on Base or Ink, an own IAllowlistChecker for pools that a willing issuer (or the demo itself) deploys, and an own ClaimIssuer for T-REX tokens the demo deploys. The user-side prerequisite in the two live attestation products (an exchange account) means a state-ID-derived attestation would also not replace the exchange's KYC in the eyes of the exchange; the earlier memo's legal analysis of AMLR retention applies.

### 4. Kill criterion

Kill the "plugs into different systems" positioning if, by the build gate G1 in [local path, withheld], the team cannot show all three of the following on a testnet without any third party's cooperation: (a) an own IAllowlistChecker wired into a PermissionsAdapter and a v4 pool that rejects a non-attested wallet and accepts an attested one; (b) an own ClaimIssuer added by addTrustedIssuer to a T-REX token whose transfer then passes isVerified for the attested wallet; (c) an own EAS schema plus indexer consumed by a contract built on the Coinbase AttestationAccessControl pattern. If only one of the three works, present the product as a single self-run consumer and do not claim reuse. Separately, drop any sentence that says a named live product "accepts" or "will accept" the attestation unless a written confirmation from that product exists; today none does.
