# Research notes: investor-money case

Every item is FACT from the named page on 2026-09-09 unless labelled otherwise. Quotes are verbatim.

## 1. Embedded wallets (the wallet type the case stands on)

- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/overview/embedded.md: "Self-custodial wallets built into your app for Ethereum, Solana, and other chains" (page subtitle; it adds that they are powered by secure hardware, TEEs). "Embedded wallets can be configured as non-custodial or custodial." "This means users have full custody of their wallets without needing to manage secret keys. Neither Privy nor your application ever sees the user's keys; secrets are only ever reconstituted in a secure environment under the user's control so they can sign messages or transactions." "Users can also export the key for their embedded wallet, providing an escape hatch to leave Privy at any time." Pregeneration for an email address before login is documented.
- https://docs.privy.io/security/wallet-infrastructure/architecture.md: two shares, "Enclave share" ("secured directly by the trusted execution environment") and "Auth share" ("encrypted and stored by Privy ... accessible only with valid authentication credentials"); "Neither the auth share nor the enclave share in isolation provide any information or access to the wallet."
- https://docs.privy.io/basics/react/setup.md: `embeddedWallets: { ethereum: { createOnLogin: 'users-without-wallets' } }` on `PrivyProvider`.
- https://docs.privy.io/authentication/user-authentication/login-methods/email.md: `useLoginWithEmail` from `@privy-io/react-auth` with `sendCode({email})` and `loginWithCode({code})` (one-time passcode). Privy's own modal (`login()`) is the alternative.
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/overview/chains.md: Tier 3 "Send Transactions" covers "Ethereum (including EVM-compatible networks)". Custom or local chains not addressed on this page.
- https://docs.privy.io/basics/react/advanced/configuring-evm-networks.md: "Privy embedded wallets can support any EVM-compatible chain"; custom chains via viem `defineChain`; "at minimum, you must provide the network's name and chain ID, native currency, RPC URLs, and a blockexplorer URL". `supportedChains` and `defaultChain` on the provider config. Whether anvil 31337 without a block explorer and with an http RPC works from Privy's iframe: unverified.
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/using-wallets/ethereum/send-a-transaction.md: `useSendTransaction` returns `Promise<{hash}>`; "To hide confirmation modals, set `options.uiOptions.showWalletUIs` to `false`" (modals shown by default). Java, Go and Python examples on the same page use `eip155:11155111` "Sepolia testnet", so Sepolia is a documented target for sending.
- https://docs.privy.io/recipes/send-usdc.md: Privy's documented stablecoin transfer from an embedded wallet uses `useSendTransaction` plus viem `encodeFunctionData` with `erc20Abi`; "USDC has 6 decimals".
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/connectors/ethereum/integrations/wagmi.md: "Privy is fully compatible with wagmi, and you can use wagmi's React hooks to interface with external and embedded wallets from Privy." Install `wagmi @privy-io/react-auth @privy-io/wagmi @tanstack/react-query`; `createConfig` and `WagmiProvider` come from `@privy-io/wagmi` ("allows Privy to drive wagmi's connectors state"; `reconnectOnMount` forced false). Nesting: PrivyProvider, then QueryClientProvider, then WagmiProvider. Caveat: "Privy does not currently support programmatically disconnecting a wallet via wagmi's useDisconnect hook." Whether wagmi's `injected()` connector list can be kept alongside: not addressed, unverified.
- Versions (npm registry 2026-09-09): `@privy-io/react-auth` 3.40.0 (published 2026-09-03), `@privy-io/wagmi` 4.0.17 (2026-08-31), `@privy-io/node` 0.34.0 (2026-08-28), `@privy-io/server-auth` 1.32.5 (2025-09-17, superseded by node). privy-io/examples `privy-next-wagmi` pins react-auth ^3.12.0, wagmi ^2.17.0, viem ^2.37.6, `@privy-io/wagmi` ^2.0.0 (older major than the current 4.x, so the example is not a version reference).
- Pricing, https://www.privy.io/pricing: "50K signatures and $1M transaction volume for free every month"; Free plan "0-499" MAU; "A monthly signature represents any cryptographic signature request from a Privy embedded wallet in the last 30 days", listing `eth_sendTransaction` and `personal_sign`. Feature matrix rows: "Embedded wallets", "Wallet connectors", "Delegated access to wallets", "Native gas sponsorship", "Native funding & bridging", "Custom onramps", "Webhooks", "Policy engine", "Key quorum approvals", "Custodial wallets", "Integrated fraud prevention (KYT)". The per-plan tick marks were not readable in the fetched HTML; which of these rows are on the free plan is unverified except embedded wallets and signatures, which the free-tier sentence covers.

## 2. Funding (onramp, add funds, deposits)

- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/funding/add-funds.md: "Privy provides a `useAddFunds` hook in `@privy-io/react-auth` that opens the onramp modal, a single flow covering every configured funding method." "`useAddFunds` is currently only available on React." Setup: "Enable card and bank funding methods on the Account Funding page in the Privy Dashboard." Options: `fiat` (uses the `useFiatOnramp` flow, `fiat.environment: 'sandbox' | 'production'`) and `crypto` ("Enables crypto funding with a deposit address flow", `crypto.refundAddress`, `crypto.slippageBps`). Return `{method:'crypto', status:'completed'}`. Testnet behaviour of the crypto deposit-address flow: not stated, unverified.
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/funding/fiat-onramp.md: `useFiatOnramp` ("Stripe, Meld, MoonPay, and Coinbase" routed by region); "Once enabled in the Privy Dashboard, fiat onramps support USD, EUR, AUD, and BRL through Stripe and MoonPay"; Meld needs "complete its KYB". Sandbox: "Pass `environment: 'sandbox'` to `fund` to run the flow against Stripe's test environment. No real funds move". Testnets: "Set `destination.chain` to a mainnet CAIP-2 identifier. Stripe's onramp does not support testnets, so testnet chains fail even in sandbox mode." Conclusion: a card onramp cannot be a live testnet flow; per the prize text a mocked onramp does not count.
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/funding/overview (fetched 2026-09-09): four methods, fiat deposits (VBANs), crypto deposits, onramp modal, card onramps. https://docs.privy.io/wallets/funding/crypto-deposits/overview.md: "Create persistent deposit addresses that convert incoming crypto into a target asset"; chains, testnets and fees not stated, unverified.
- privy-io/funding-demo (gh, 2026-09-09): `lib/privy/FundingButton.tsx` calls `fundWallet(wallets[0].address, { asset: "USDC", amount: "15" })` from `useFundWallet` on react-auth ^2.4.2 (old major). privy-io/examples `privy-next-funding` (react-auth ^3.35.2) has `sections/fund-wallet.tsx`. Neither shows a testnet run.
- Testnet USDC: Circle lists Ethereum Sepolia USDC at `0x1c7D4B196Cb0C7B01d743Fbc6116a902379C7238` (https://developers.circle.com/stablecoins/usdc-contract-addresses, fetched 2026-09-09); https://faucet.circle.com/: "Developers can request 20 USDC on testnet every 2 hours, per address, and per blockchain", Ethereum Sepolia listed.

## 3. Transfers, bridging, stablecoin conversion (the managed wallet action)

- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/transfer/overview.md: well-known assets `usdc, eth, sol, pol, usdt, eurc`; custom ERC20 needs the dashboard "Asset watchlist"; supported chains table includes "Ethereum Sepolia | eip155:11155111 | ethereum_sepolia", Base Sepolia, Arbitrum Sepolia. "the transfer API supports setting different USD stablecoins (USDC, USDT, USDC.e, USDT0) as the source and destination currency." Bridging via Relay (https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/transfer/bridging, WebSearch result 2026-09-09): "Bridge fills typically complete within seconds on mainnet"; testnet routes unverified.
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/transfer/usage.md: "To execute a transfer, make a `POST` request to `/v1/wallets/{wallet_id}/transfer`." Node SDK `privy.wallets().transfer()`; authorization via `authorization_context` (authorization private keys or user JWTs). No React hook on the page; a `useTransfer` hook was not found in the docs or by WebSearch (2026-09-09). So the managed transfer action is a server-side call on a wallet id; for a user's embedded wallet it needs a signer or a user JWT in the authorization context (https://docs.privy.io/recipes/wallets/server-side-user-wallets.md: "request an ephemeral user key", `privy-authorization-signature` header).
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/transfer/policies.md: policies can constrain `source.asset`, `source.amount`, `source.chain`, `destination.address` (allowlist), `destination.chain`, `destination.asset`.

## 4. Swaps

- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/swap/overview.md: EVM testnets "Unichain Sepolia, Monad Testnet, Robinhood Testnet, Sepolia, Base Sepolia"; "Gas sponsorship is required for swaps, meaning Privy sponsors these fees from your app's gas credits"; Privy fee "up to 0.25% on each swap"; routing through the Uniswap API (https://blog.uniswap.org/privy-integrates-uniswap-api, CLAIM).
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/swap/setup.md: "Toggle Enable token swaps ... Swaps are disabled by default." "Gas sponsorship must be enabled for your app before swaps can execute ... ensure your gas credits are funded." Not used here: a Privy swap routes through Privy's aggregator, not through the permissioned pool, so the eligibility gate would not be exercised (same reasoning as spec-privy.md).

## 6. Gas sponsorship, TEE execution, policies, webhooks (for the B2B question)

- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/gas-and-asset-management/gas/setup.md: "App pays" (gas credits) or "User pays" (USDC or USDT balance pays gas; Sepolia and Base Sepolia in the table). "Apps must use TEE execution in order to use our native gas sponsorship feature." How credits are obtained or whether testnet gas is free: not stated, unverified.
- https://docs.privy.io/recipes/tee-wallet-migration-guide.md: "Your app must enable TEE execution in order to access the following features: ... Policy engine in order to restrict Ethereum and Solana transactions. Server-side access to wallets, using signers." Dashboard shows "On-device (with an option to Request access to migrate to TEE)" or "TEE enabled"; "TEE execution is supported by ... React: @privy-io/react-auth@3.13.0" or later. Whether a new self-serve app starts TEE-enabled: unverified.
- https://docs.privy.io/controls/policies/overview.md: "You can create and manage policies through the Privy Dashboard, nodeJS SDK, or via the REST API." Default `DENY` when no rule resolves; conditions on recipients, calldata, chain id, value. Plan restriction not stated on the page; pricing matrix row exists (tick marks unreadable): unverified whether the free plan includes it.
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/actions/webhooks.md: "Webhooks can be tested at no cost in development environments. To enable webhooks in production, upgrade to the Enterprise plan".
- https://docs.privy.io/wallets/using-wallets/signers/overview.md: signers "can be added to wallets owned by users, authorization keys, or key quorums"; "a signer will never see the wallet's private key".

## 8. Regulatory and market facts used in "otherwise not possible"

- raw/2026-09-06-live-gated-products.md:186 (FACT from the memo, COUNT of the memo's survey): tokenized-stock and fund secondaries "do not check eligibility for secondary holders at all ... their primary market is issuer KYC with a wallet whitelist for mint and redeem." No live product accepts an external attestation without a contract.
- raw/2026-09-06-product-pitch-legal.md:41: Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 (Travel Rule) applies from 2024-12-30; for transfers above EUR 1,000 to or from a self-hosted address the CASP "shall take adequate measures to assess whether that address is owned or controlled by" the originator or beneficiary (Art 14(5), Art 16(2)); EBA/GL/2024/11 paragraph 83(d) accepts "requesting the customer to digitally sign a specific message ... with the key corresponding to that address". AMLR 2024/1624 Art 40 adds risk measures for self-hosted transfers from 2027-07-10.
- Same memo :138 to :139: a predicate never replaces AMLR Art 22(1)(a) data and Art 77 retention for an obliged entity; the EUDI wallet changes the verification step (Art 22(6)(b)), not the retention step. The case keeps the issuer's compliance file out of scope and out of the chain, as product.md requires.
